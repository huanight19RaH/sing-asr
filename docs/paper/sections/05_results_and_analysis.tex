Statistical testing confirms that the adapted champion model delivers a four-fold improvement in singing tone preservation accuracy ($47.53\%$ vs. $11.54\%$, $\chi^2 = 119.15, p = 9.73 \times 10^{-28}$, Odds Ratio $= 6.94$) \cite{pearson1900x2,bland2000odds}. Moreover, tone deletions in singing are reduced ten-fold relative to spoken anchors ($8.64\%$ vs. $48.72\%, \chi^2 = 87.56, p = 8.19 \times 10^{-21}$, Odds Ratio $= 0.10$).

In the singing confusion matrix, Level tone (\textit{Ngang}) exhibits $56.3\%$ recall, with confusion concentrating along Falling (\textit{Huy\`en}, $22.9\%$) and Dot (\textit{N\d{a}ng}, $8.6\%$). Falling tone achieves $50.0\%$ recall, with residual errors gravitating toward Level ($30.8\%$) and Rising (\textit{S\'ac}, $11.5\%$). This empirical distribution confirms our theoretical hypothesis: musical melody pulls rising and falling tones into flat pitch registers, driving bidirectional substitutions between Level and Falling tones.

\subsection{2D Cross-Attention Heatmap Dynamics and Inference Latency}
We extract the cross-attention alignment matrix $A_{t, f}$ across decoder layers. We evaluate the Monotonicity Index $r = \text{Corr}(t, \arg\max_f A_{t, f})$ using Pearson correlation \cite{pearson1895notes}. For baseline PhoWhisper, $r = 0.2512$ ($p = 0.1282$, statistically non-significant), reflecting severe horizontal stalling and loop traps. In contrast, our champion model achieves $r = 0.4531$ with high statistical significance ($p = 0.00486 < 0.01$), demonstrating that our distillation objective enforces steady monotonic diagonal tracking across sustained musical vowels.

The complete system operates at a Real-Time Factor (RTF) of $0.0384$ on an NVIDIA T4 GPU, processing 10 seconds of audio in under 385 milliseconds ($26\times$ faster than real-time), confirming practical suitability for real-time lyrics transcription.

\begin{abstract}
Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) foundation models achieve remarkable accuracy on spoken prose but degrade catastrophically on singing voice. In tonal languages such as Vietnamese, this degradation is intensified by two coupled phenomena: (1) \textit{Melodic Tone Neutralization}, wherein musical melodic pitch ($F_0$) overrules lexical pitch contours, inducing severe phonetic-tonal confusion; and (2) \textit{Attention Collapse}, where sustained vibrato vowels trap autoregressive decoders in horizontal attention stalling and infinite repetition loops. 
To resolve these challenges without costly multi-thousand-hour retraining, we propose a progressive three-tier adaptation framework: (i) front-end non-linear acoustic conditioning combining transient consonant boosting and melody-tone decoupling; (ii) parameter-efficient Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) covering both attention projections and feed-forward MLPs ($2.78\%$ parameter budget); and (iii) Monotonic Cross-Attention Knowledge Distillation incorporating adaptive Gaussian diagonality penalties and centroid progression regularization.
We validate our framework on a unified multi-domain benchmark encompassing 58 real, non-synthetic audio clips across four contrasting domains: Vietnamese singing voice, Mozilla Common Voice 27.0, VietSuperSpeech conversational speech, and VIVOS studio speech.
Empirical results demonstrate that our champion model slashes singing Word Error Rate (WER) from $462.18\%$ (baseline loop explosion) down to $100.43\%$ ($7.69\%$ on clean melodic phrases), reducing spurious insertions by $90.6\%$ (from 524 to 49). 
A comprehensive 6-tone Levenshtein confusion analysis confirms a four-fold gain in singing tonal accuracy ($47.53\%$ vs. $11.54\%$, $\chi^2 = 119.15, p = 9.73 \times 10^{-28}$, Odds Ratio $= 6.94$). 
Furthermore, 2D cross-attention extraction reveals that our distillation restores sharp monotonic diagonal alignment ($r = 0.4531, p = 0.00486$) over baseline attention stagnation ($r = 0.2512, p = 0.128$), running at a Real-Time Factor (RTF) of $0.0384$ ($26\times$ real-time).
\end{abstract}

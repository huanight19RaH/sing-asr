\section{Introduction}
Modern sequence-to-sequence foundation models, such as OpenAI Whisper \cite{radford2023robust} and PhoWhisper \cite{phan2022phowhisper}, have fundamentally transformed automatic speech recognition across diverse global languages. However, their transcription accuracy suffers a dramatic, catastrophic drop when exposed to singing voice---even when sung lyrics are linguistically identical to standard conversational utterances \cite{gupta2022deep,zhuo2023lyricwhiz}. This persistent performance gap between speech and song is formally designated as the \textit{Speech-to-Song Mismatch} \cite{ou2022transfer,mesaros2010automatic}.

In tonal languages such as Vietnamese, Mandarin, and Cantonese, this mismatch poses severe linguistic and computational challenges \cite{pham2003vietnamese,nguyen1997vietnamese,wong2002how}. Unlike non-tonal languages where fundamental frequency primarily conveys pragmatic affect or sentential intonation, Vietnamese assigns core lexical contrasts to six canonical tones: \textit{Ngang} (Level), \textit{Huy\`en} (Falling), \textit{S\'ac} (Rising), \textit{H\H{o}i} (Hook/Dipping), \textit{Ng\~a} (Tilde/Glottalized Broken), and \textit{N\d{a}ng} (Dot/Constricted Drop) \cite{brunelle2009tone,kirby2016tone}. These tones are phonologically distinguished by dynamic fundamental frequency contours ($F_0(t)$), duration, and phonation registers (creaky voice, glottal closure). 

When an individual sings, the musical melody dictates vocal cord vibration according to discrete musical scale degrees (e.g., C-E-G), overriding natural lexical pitch contours \cite{kirby2016tone}. This acoustic collision produces \textit{Melodic Tone Neutralization}, wherein syllables with rising or falling lexical tones are flattened into level melodic sustains, causing standard ASR acoustic decoders to commit severe phoneme and tone misclassifications \cite{vu2024vietsing,hoang2026vietprism}.

Concurrently, singing voice features prolonged vowel durations (stretching up to $300\text{--}500\%$ relative to conversational speech) and slow periodic vibrato pitch modulations ($5\text{--}8$ Hz) \cite{mesaros2010automatic,berendes2024lyrics}. Under standard Transformer cross-attention architectures \cite{vaswani2017attention,bahdanau2015neural}, these sustained acoustic frames cause cross-attention matrices to stall horizontally, as the autoregressive decoder repeatedly queries identical acoustic states \cite{stoller2024jamalt}. Unable to detect expected phonetic transitions, the decoder degenerates into runaway repetition loops, inflating Word Error Rates (WER) past several hundred percent due to spurious token insertions \cite{morris2004wer,levenshtein1966binary}.

To overcome these structural barriers without the prohibitive cost of multi-thousand-hour pre-training from scratch, we propose a progressive three-tier adaptation framework tailored for tonal singing voice:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Acoustic Determinants \& 6-Tone Error Taxonomy:} We establish a fine-grained Levenshtein alignment framework evaluating all six Vietnamese tones across a verified 58-item multi-domain benchmark testbed spanning four contrasting domains (Vietnamese singing voice, Mozilla Common Voice 27.0 \cite{ardila2020common}, VietSuperSpeech conversational speech \cite{thanh2026vietsuperspeech}, and VIVOS studio speech \cite{luong2016vivos}). We quantify the complete tonal confusion matrix and prove with extreme statistical significance ($\chi^2 = 119.15, p = 9.73 \times 10^{-28}$) that musical singing alters tone preservation dynamics compared to speech.
    \item \textbf{2D Cross-Attention Diagnostics \& Distillation:} We demonstrate empirically that autoregressive repetition loops stem directly from horizontal cross-attention stagnation. We introduce an adaptive Gaussian diagonality and centroid progression distillation objective \cite{tachibana2018efficiently,he2019monotonic,hinton2015distilling} that enforces monotonic alignment ($r = 0.4531, p = 0.00486$).
    \item \textbf{Deep Bias-Variance Regularized LoRA:} By extending Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) \cite{hu2021lora,mangrulkar2022peft} across both multi-head attention and feed-forward MLP projections, coupled with L1 parameter sparsity, decoupled AdamW weight decay \cite{loshchilov2018decoupled}, and label smoothing \cite{szegedy2016rethinking}, our champion model reduces spurious singing insertions by $90.6\%$ and achieves an inference speed of $26\times$ real-time ($RTF = 0.0384$).
\end{enumerate}

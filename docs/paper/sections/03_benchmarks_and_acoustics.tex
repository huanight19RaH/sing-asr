\subsection{16-Variable Acoustic Feature Extraction}
For each benchmark utterance, we extract a comprehensive 16-dimensional acoustic vector utilizing Librosa \cite{mcfee2015librosa} and Praat via Parselmouth \cite{boersma2002praat,jadoul2018parselmouth}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pitch Dynamics ($F_0$):} Fundamental frequency mean, standard deviation, coefficient of variation ($CV_{F_0} = \sigma_{F_0}/\mu_{F_0}$), semitone pitch dynamic range, and vibrato modulation frequency (Hz).
    \item \textbf{Temporal Pacing:} Speaking rate (syllables per second) and total voiced duration.
    \item \textbf{Formant Resonances \& Vocal Tract Geometry:} First three formant central frequencies ($F_1, F_2, F_3$) and formant dispersion $\Delta F_{\text{disp}} = (F_3 - F_1)/2$ \cite{fitch1997vocal,fant1970acoustic}.
    \item \textbf{Voice Quality \& Phonation:} Harmonics-to-Noise Ratio (HNR in dB) \cite{yumoto1982harmonics}, spectral tilt, local jitter (\%), and local shimmer (\%) \cite{farrus2007jitter}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Instrument-Specific Acoustic Collision Profiling}
To isolate the destructive physical mechanisms induced by specific musical instruments in polyphonic arrangements, we formulate four diagnostic collision descriptors:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Transient Energy Index (TEI):} The high-frequency ($>2.5$\,kHz) crest factor and onset transient burst density, measuring consonant onset masking caused by percussive drum/snare hits.
    \item \textbf{Formant Disturbance Ratio (FDR):} The normalized Frobenius spectral deviation within the vocal formant band ($300\text{--}2,500$\,Hz), quantifying formant smearing produced by dense polyphonic piano chords.
    \item \textbf{Pitch Tracking Deviation (PTD):} The frame ratio of pitch errors ($>1$ semitone deviation) induced by competing harmonic sources, such as guitar string vibrato ($4\text{--}7$\,Hz) and pitch bends.
    \item \textbf{Spectral Energy Floor (SEF):} The proportion of total spectral power concentrated in the sub-bass floor ($<150$\,Hz), characterizing dynamic range saturation caused by 808 synth bass lines.
\end{enumerate}

Bivariate correlation analysis confirms that fundamental frequency semitone range correlates significantly with word deletions ($r = 0.3345, p = 0.0103$; Spearman $\rho = 0.3433, p = 0.0083$) \cite{pearson1895notes,spearman1904proof}. Similarly, vibrato modulation rate correlates positively with token deletions ($r = 0.3445, p = 0.0081$; $\rho = 0.2675, p = 0.0424$). As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:tone_confusion}, these empirical relationships demonstrate that rapid melodic frequency modulations blur acoustic frame representations, prompting the decoder to drop tokens prematurely. Conversely, our regularized champion model exhibits an essentially flat correlation with utterance duration ($r = -0.0412, p = 0.7589$), confirming that it is insulated from length-dependent insertion blowups.

\subsection{Multi-Seed Robustness Validation Protocol}
To ensure that empirical performance gains are statistically robust and not artefacts of favorable pseudo-random parameter initialization, we implement a multi-seed evaluation protocol across ten distinct random seeds: $S = \{42, 123, 456, 789, 2026, 11, 77, 999, 1337, 2027\}$. For each seed $s \in S$, network adapters and sampling routines are reinitialized independently. We report the unbiased sample mean ($\mu$), sample standard deviation ($\sigma$), and two-sided $95\%$ Confidence Intervals based on Student's $t$-distribution with degrees of freedom $df = N - 1 = 9$:
\begin{equation}
\text{CI}_{95\%} = \left[ \mu - t_{0.025, 9} \cdot \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{N}}, \; \mu + t_{0.025, 9} \cdot \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{N}} \right]
\end{equation}
where critical threshold $t_{0.025, 9} = 2.262$. We evaluate the inter-seed Coefficient of Variation ($CV = \frac{\sigma}{\mu} \times 100\%$) to quantify stochastic variance and compute paired two-tailed Student's $t$-tests comparing proposed models against unmitigated baselines across all test suites.
